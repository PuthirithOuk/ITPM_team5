# PRINCE2 7 comparison · Realtime Quiz hybrid governance

## Concept mapping

| # | Hybrid life cycle element | PRINCE2 7 equivalent | Description and role comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sponsor (Head of IT) | **Project Board / Executive** | Owns the business justification, controls project funding, and decides at stage boundaries whether the project proceeds. |
| 2 | Project Manager (Lecturer) | **Project Manager** | Manages day-to-day governance, enforces tolerances, prepares stage plans, and acts as the interface between the team and sponsor. |
| 3 | Product Owner (Student B) | **Senior User** | Specifies user requirements, prioritizes the product backlog based on customer value, and verifies user acceptance criteria. |
| 4 | Developers (Students A–D) | **Team Members / Team Manager** | Produces deliverables to the agreed technical specifications and Definition of Done within timeboxes. |
| 5 | Two-week Agile Sprint | **Work Package / Delivery Stage** | A defined, bounded package of authorized work delivered within allocated time and quality constraints. |
| 6 | Stage Gates (G1–G4) | **End Stage Assessment** | Formal review point at the end of a management stage where the Project Board evaluates performance and approves the next stage plan. |
| 7 | Product Backlog Item / Story | **Product Description** | Formally documents what will be produced, its quality criteria, tolerances, and acceptance method. |
| 8 | Definition of Done | **Quality Register & Criteria** | Measurable quality checklist that every product must satisfy before being accepted as complete. |
| 9 | Weekly Highlight Report | **Highlight Report** | Periodic status report sent from the PM to the Project Board summarizing stage progress, tolerances, and impending risks. |
| 10 | Exception Trigger | **Exception Report & Exception Plan** | Formal report raised by the PM when a stage tolerance is forecast to be exceeded, detailing corrective options for Board approval. |
| 11 | Pilot and Hand-over (G4) | **Closing a Project** | Formal decommissioning process verifying acceptance, handing over operational assets, and documenting lessons learned. |

## What PRINCE2 would add that this governance lacks

PRINCE2 would introduce continuous, formal **Business Case validation** at every stage boundary. In our student hybrid setup, the business justification is largely taken as given once approved in week 3 at G1. Under PRINCE2, the Project Board explicitly re-evaluates whether the project remains viable, desirable, and achievable at G2 and G3 before authorizing further expenditure. 

Furthermore, PRINCE2 mandates structured **Issue and Change Control Management** with an independent Change Authority and formal risk budgets. Any modification to a baselined product description requires formal impact analysis and recorded approval, preventing undocumented requirements drift.

---

## What PRINCE2 would make heavier

For a four-student academic team with 10 hours per week per student, full PRINCE2 compliance would introduce crippling documentation and administrative overhead:
1. Formulating full **Product Descriptions** for dozens of individual backlog items (including derivation, format, quality criteria, and quality tolerances) would consume tens of hours that are needed for software development.
2. Formally drafting **Work Package Agreements**, maintaining an **Issue Register**, logging formal **Quality Review minutes**, and assembling comprehensive **End Stage Reports** for four consecutive gates would overwhelm the PM’s 2 h/week supervision allowance. 

In a low-budget (200 USD) internal academic project, the excessive process bureaucracy of pure PRINCE2 would exhaust team capacity without providing proportional risk-reduction value.
